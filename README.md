💼 HR Attrition & Engagement Dashboard

## 📘 Project Overview
This project analyzes employee attrition and engagement using **SQL Server** for data preparation and **Power BI** for visualization.  
It explores key HR metrics like satisfaction, salary, promotion, and working hours to understand why employees leave and how to improve retention.

---

## 🧮 Tools Used
- **SQL Server** – for cleaning, transforming, and exploring data  
- **Power BI** – for creating an interactive dashboard  
- **DAX** – for custom measures and percentage KPIs  

---

## 📊 Dashboard Highlights
### KPIs
- 👥 **Employees Left:** 1,991  
- 👩‍💼 **Total Employees:** 11,991  
- 📉 **Attrition Rate:** 17%  
- 😊 **Average Satisfaction:** 63%  
- ⭐ **Average Evaluation:** 72%  

### Visuals
- **Attrition by Department** – shows highest attrition in HR (19%) and Accounting (18%)  
- **Attrition by Salary Level** – low-salary employees form over 50% of total attrition  
- **Satisfaction vs Attrition** – employees who left have only 44% average satisfaction compared to 67% who stayed  
- **Working Hours Distribution** – most employees work around 150–250 hours per month  
- **Slicers:** Salary, Department, Promotion (last 5 years)

---

## 💡 Key Insights
1. **High attrition (17%)** mainly occurs among **low-salary employees** and **non-promoted staff**.  
2. Employees who left show **much lower satisfaction (44%)** than those who stayed (67%).  
3. **HR and Accounting** departments experience the **highest turnover**, indicating possible job dissatisfaction or workload imbalance.  
4. Working hours above **250/month** correlate with higher attrition, suggesting overwork as a factor.  
5. Improving satisfaction and offering promotions can significantly reduce attrition.

---

## 🧠 Conclusion
This analysis helps HR managers identify the key factors behind employee turnover and focus on areas such as **work-life balance**, **salary structure**, and **career development** to boost retention.
